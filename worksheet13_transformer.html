Part 1 · 2014
The RNN breaks
1

An RNN cannot translate

Problem 1 of 9

Can the RNN we built translate “I am reading a book” into Hindi?

ProblemThe RNN writes one Hindi word at each step, in step with the English words. Hindi needs six words, in a different order.

Key ideaTranslation does not go word by word in order, so the output cannot be produced in step with the input.

Fix · Read everything, then write

FixUse two RNNs. The encoder reads the whole English sentence. The decoder then writes the Hindi sentence, one word at a time.

Key ideaLengths no longer need to match, and nothing is needed from the future.

2

One vector carries the whole sentence

Problem 2 of 9

The encoder-decoder works on short sentences. Now give it a long one.

ProblemThe decoder sees the English sentence only through one context vector of fixed size.

Key ideaOne fixed-size vector, carried through many steps, cannot hold a long sentence.

Which English word does किताब need?

Think first. The decoder is about to write किताब (kitaab). Which English word does it need? Show the answer
“book”. So why force the decoder to dig it out of a summary of the whole sentence? Let it look at “book” directly.

First attempt: move in lockstep

Fix · Let the decoder look back

FixKeep every encoder state. At each step, the decoder looks back at all of them and takes a weighted mix. This is attention.

  1. ScoreCompare the decoder’s current state with every encoder state.
  2. SoftmaxTurn the scores into weights that add up to 1.
  3. Weighted sumMix the encoder states with those weights, and hand the mix to the decoder.

Key ideaEvery output word gets its own view of the input.

The model learns where to look

Nobody tells the model which words match. It is trained only to translate, and it finds the alignment because looking at the right word helps. Here is a real alignment from Bahdanau’s paper, from English to French.

Think first. Find “European Economic Area”. In which order does the model look at these three words? Show the answer
In reverse. French says “zone économique européenne”, so the model looks at “Area”, then “Economic”, then “European”. Attention handles the change of order by itself.

Part 2 · 2016
The bold move
3

The encoder is still an RNN

Problem 3 of 9

Attention fixed how the decoder sees the input. The encoder still reads one word at a time.

ProblemDistant words are linked only through a long chain of steps, and each step must wait for the one before.

Key ideaLong chains fade, and waiting in line leaves the GPU idle.

Story · Google · 2016

The heretic

Jakob UszkoreitGoogle Research

He believed attention alone, with no recurrence at all, could beat recurrent networks. It meant throwing out every existing architecture. Even his father, a well-known computational linguist, disagreed with him over dinner.

Illia PolosukhinGoogle Research · the lunch

He complained that answers on Google search had to come back within milliseconds, and RNNs were too slow. Uszkoreit’s answer: why not use self-attention?

Fix · Every word attends to every word

FixRemove recurrence. In self-attention, every word looks at every other word directly, all at the same time.

What self-attention produces

Each word takes a weighted mix of the words around it. So the same word can end up with different meanings in different sentences.

Key ideaThe same word starts with the same embedding. After self-attention, it carries its meaning in context.

What did we just lose?

Recurrence did four jobs for us without being asked. Self-attention threw them away.

Think first. Name the four jobs. Show the answer

  2. It processed each word through a nonlinear step.
  3. It could be trained, with the help of the LSTM, without falling apart.
  4. When writing, it could see only the words already written.

4

Relevance runs one way

Problem 4 of 9

Self-attention needs a score for every pair of words. The simplest score compares their two embeddings.

Problem“it” needs “animal”, but “animal” has no need for “it”. Comparing embeddings gives both directions the same score, and every word matches itself best.

Fix · Three roles for every word

FixEach word produces three vectors, each from its own learned transformation.

Query

What this word is looking for.

Key

What this word offers, so other words can find it.

Value

The information this word passes on once it is chosen.

The query of “it” is compared with the key of “animal”. The query of “animal” is compared with the key of “it”. These are different vectors, so the two directions get different scores.

The full recipe for one word

Key ideaEvery word runs this recipe at the same time. The new “it” is mostly “animal”.

The trained Transformer does exactly this. In the paper’s model, “its” looks at the noun it refers to.

5

Word order is gone

Problem 5 of 9Wins back: word order

Recurrence always knew where each word sat. Self-attention does not.

ProblemSelf-attention never looks at positions. Shuffle the words, and each word’s output stays exactly the same.

FixAdd a position vector to each word’s embedding before attention. The paper builds these vectors from sine and cosine waves, so every position gets its own pattern.

Key ideaNow “dog” at position 1 and “dog” at position 3 start out different, and attention can use that difference.

Story · Google · 2017

Good, but not better

The early Transformer models worked. They matched the best LSTM systems but, in Uszkoreit’s words, they were not better. The team needed someone to turn a promising idea into a system that wins.

Noam ShazeerGoogle, since 2000

Walking past a colleague’s desk, he overheard Vaswani and Parmar discussing self-attention. He joined, rewrote the code himself, and came back saying it worked. The paper credits him with scaled dot-product attention, multi-head attention and the position representation.

6

One average cannot hold every relationship

Problem 6 of 9

A word has several relationships at once. “it” refers to “animal”, “it” is described by “tired”, and “didn’t” belongs with “cross”.

ProblemOne attention computes one weighted average, so every relationship blends into it.

FixRun several attention heads side by side, each with its own query, key and value transformations. Then join their outputs. The paper uses 8 heads.

Key ideaEach head learns its own relationship.

7

Attention only mixes

Problem 7 of 9Wins back: processing

In the RNN, every step passed the word through a nonlinear transformation.

ProblemThe output of attention is a weighted average of value vectors. Information moves between words, but no word is transformed.

FixAfter attention, pass each word through a small feed-forward network. The same network is applied to every position separately.

Key ideaAttention is communication between words. The feed-forward network is processing within each word.

8

Deep stacks are hard to train

Problem 8 of 9Wins back: depth

One block is not enough. The Transformer stacks six.

ProblemIn training, the gradient must flow back through every block, and it shrinks on the way.

FixWrap every sub-layer in Add & Norm. The skip connection adds the input back to the output, so the gradient has a direct path. Layer norm keeps the values in a steady range.

You have seen this idea before. The LSTM’s long-term memory also adds instead of rewriting, and that is why its learning signal survived long gaps.

Key ideaAdd, do not replace. The gradient reaches every block.

The encoder block, complete

Part 4 · 2017
The decoder
9

The decoder can see the answer

Problem 9 of 9Wins back: no peeking

The decoder writes Hindi one word at a time. In training, it receives the whole correct Hindi sentence at once, so that it can learn every position in parallel.

ProblemSelf-attention lets every position see every word, including the future words it is supposed to predict.

FixMask the future. Before softmax, every future cell is blocked, so its weight becomes 0.

Key ideaEach position sees only the words already written.
